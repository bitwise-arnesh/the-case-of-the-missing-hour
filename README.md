## 📖 About the Project

**The Case of the Missing Hour** is an interactive, command-based detective mystery game developed in Python as my final project for the **Stanford Code in Place (CIP) program**.

The game places the player in the role of a detective investigating a mysterious murder at an old ancestral mansion near Shantiniketan, West Bengal, India. Through exploration, evidence collection, suspect interrogation, and deduction, the player must uncover the truth behind a seemingly impossible locked-room murder.

The project combines traditional Python programming with AI-powered interactions using the `call_gpt()` function provided by the Code in Place environment.

Rather than being a simple chatbot, the game uses Python to control its world, clues, suspects, inventory, investigation progression, scoring, and outcomes. AI is integrated to make conversations more dynamic and to assist with evidence analysis and narrative endings.

## 🎭 The Story

### Chapter 1 — The Storm

It is the night of **15 August 2026**.

Heavy monsoon rain falls over the countryside near Shantiniketan. Hidden among trees, ponds, and narrow village roads stands **Roy Chowdhury Bari**, an old ancestral mansion belonging to Dr. Arindam Roy Chowdhury.

Dr. Arindam is a respected engineer, entrepreneur, and inventor. His life's work is **Project Surya**, a revolutionary energy-storage technology that could have enormous commercial and scientific value.

At approximately 11:05 PM, a frantic call reports that Dr. Arindam has been found dead inside his study.

The storm has isolated the mansion.

The study door was reportedly locked from the inside. The windows appear undisturbed. No obvious escape route can be found.

But one detail catches the detective's attention.

The clock inside the study has stopped at exactly:

**10:17 PM.**

The murder appears to have occurred during a mysterious missing hour in which the events inside the mansion remain uncertain.

Four people were present that evening.

Everyone has a story.

Everyone has something to hide.

And one of them is a murderer.

The game takes place entirely around **Roy Chowdhury Bari**, a fictional ancestral mansion near Shantiniketan in West Bengal.

The setting incorporates an atmosphere inspired by:

- Monsoon nights in Bengal
- Old ancestral houses

## 👥 The Suspects

There are four suspects. Each has a motive, an alibi, a personality, and secrets that may make them appear guilty.

### 1. Ananya Roy Chowdhury — The Wife

**Relationship:** Wife of the victim

Ananya's marriage to Dr. Arindam had become strained. Their relationship was no longer what it once was, and she had reportedly been considering leaving him.

There were also discussions regarding changes to the family will.

She claims that she spent the evening praying in the puja room.

**Motive:** A deteriorating marriage and possible inheritance-related tensions.

**Alibi:** Claims she was in the puja room.

**Secret:** She was planning to leave her husband and had discussed changes to the family will.

Her incomplete alibi and personal circumstances make her suspicious, but suspicion alone does not establish guilt.

---

### 2. Suman Das — The Caretaker

**Relationship:** Long-time caretaker of Roy Chowdhury Bari

Suman has served the family for nearly thirty years. He knows the mansion better than almost anyone, including its old corridors, service areas, and architectural secrets.

He claims that he was preparing tea in the kitchen around the time of the murder.

**Motive:** His knowledge of the mansion gives him access to places others may not know exist.

**Alibi:** Claims he was in the kitchen preparing tea.

**Secret:** Suman knew about the mansion's hidden service passage but had concealed its existence because he feared it would be destroyed during renovations.

He is evasive about certain details, but he is not the murderer.

---

### 3. Rohan Sen — The Business Partner

**Relationship:** Business partner of Dr. Arindam

Rohan is intelligent, charming, confident, and deeply involved in Project Surya.

He claims he was reading in the library during the murder.

Initially, he appears to be one of the most trustworthy people in the mansion.

However, financial records and laboratory access logs begin to reveal inconsistencies in his story.

**Motive:** Financial gain and access to confidential Project Surya research.

**Alibi:** Claims he was in the library.

**Secret:** Rohan was secretly selling Project Surya research to Meridian Tech Pvt. Ltd., a company based in Mumbai.

He had received substantial payments without the knowledge of the other stakeholders.

Dr. Arindam discovered the betrayal and planned to expose Rohan the following morning.

Rohan had much more to lose than he initially admitted.

---

### 4. Dr. Meera Banerjee — The Scientist

**Relationship:** Research scientist involved in Project Surya

Meera worked closely with Dr. Arindam on Project Surya.

However, she was recently removed from the patent application, resulting in a heated confrontation between them.

She is brilliant, outspoken, frustrated, and emotionally invested in the project.

**Motive:** Professional betrayal and loss of recognition.

**Alibi:** Claims she was reviewing research documents.

**Secret:** Meera was furious about being removed from the patent application and confronted Dr. Arindam earlier that evening.

Her anger makes her a plausible suspect, but she did not murder him.

# 🔎 Investigation Locations

The player can investigate different areas of the mansion. Each location contains a fixed piece of evidence that contributes to solving the mystery.

## 1. The Study

The scene of the murder.

The study contains scattered papers, a disturbed desk, an overturned chair, and a clock frozen at 10:17 PM.

### Evidence: The Broken Watch

A broken wristwatch lies near the desk.

Its hands are frozen at 10:17 PM.

A torn document contains the surviving words:

- Technology Transfer
- Mumbai
- R.S.

A faint patch of machine oil is also found near the desk.

These details suggest that the murder may be connected to business dealings and technical research.

---

## 2. The Library

The library contains hundreds of books, financial documents, and a cabinet that appears to have been disturbed.

### Evidence: The Financial Ledger

A financial ledger reveals suspicious payments made to **Meridian Tech Pvt. Ltd.**, a company in Mumbai.

The payments were authorized by Rohan Sen.

The amounts are unusually large and appear to have been transferred without the knowledge of the other Project Surya stakeholders.

This clue introduces the possibility of financial betrayal.

---

## 3. Project Surya Laboratory

The laboratory contains experimental batteries, confidential technical documents, electrical equipment, and a computer terminal.

### Evidence: Laboratory Access Records

The access records reveal that Rohan Sen entered the laboratory after normal working hours.

He accessed confidential Project Surya files.

The machine oil found near the study is consistent with oil used to maintain laboratory equipment.

The evidence links Rohan to the research and provides a possible connection to the crime scene.

---

## 4. The Puja Room

The puja room contains brass lamps, incense, flowers, and a family photograph.

Ananya claims that she spent much of the evening praying here.

### Evidence: The Incomplete Alibi

The room shows evidence of recent use.

However, the evidence cannot establish that Ananya remained in the room continuously throughout the entire period of the murder.

Her alibi is incomplete.

This makes her timeline questionable, but an incomplete alibi is not proof of murder.

---

## 5. The Kitchen

The kitchen is quiet, with a cup of tea left on the counter.

Suman claims he was preparing tea around 10:00 PM.

### Evidence: The Untouched Tea

The tea prepared for Dr. Arindam remains untouched.

The tea appears to have been prepared earlier than Suman's claimed timeline suggests.

This raises questions about his account of the evening.

However, the clue alone does not prove that Suman is responsible.

---

## 6. The Courtyard

The courtyard is soaked by monsoon rain.

Mud has collected along the stone path, and a lantern flickers near the rear gate.

### Evidence: The Muddy Footprints

Muddy footprints lead from the courtyard toward the rear service corridor.

They appear to have been made recently.

The footprints suggest that someone moved through the courtyard during the storm.

---

## 7. The Hidden Service Passage

Behind an old wooden panel lies a narrow passage built decades ago for servants to move quietly through the mansion.

The passage connects parts of the house that appear inaccessible from the main corridors.

### Evidence: The Hidden Passage

The passage connects the study to the rear service corridor.

A person familiar with the mansion could enter or leave the study without using its main door.

This discovery challenges the apparent locked-room mystery.

The study may not have been as impossible to enter or leave as it first appeared.

---

## 8. Rohan's Guest Room

Rohan's guest room appears unusually tidy.

His suitcase rests near the wardrobe, and technical documents are scattered across the desk.

Something about the room suggests hurried activity.

### Evidence: The Missing USB Drive

A compartment in Rohan's suitcase contains an empty USB-drive case labelled:

**PROJECT SURYA — CONFIDENTIAL**

The drive itself is missing.

The research may have been copied or removed before Dr. Arindam's death.

This clue connects Rohan to the confidential technology at the centre of the investigation.

---

# 🧩 The Mystery

The murder initially appears to be impossible.

Dr. Arindam was found inside a locked study.

The doors were apparently secured from within.

The windows were undisturbed.

There was no obvious escape route.

The clock stopped at 10:17 PM.

The player must investigate whether the apparent timeline and locked-room circumstances are genuine—or whether someone manipulated the scene.

The mystery is solved by connecting evidence rather than relying on one suspicious person or one isolated clue.

# 🎮 Gameplay

The player interacts with the game using text-based commands.

## Available Commands

| Command | Function |
|---|---|
| `help` | Displays the command guide |
| `locations` | Lists all investigation locations |
| `suspects` | Displays the four suspects |
| `investigate <location>` | Investigates a location and discovers evidence |
| `interrogate <suspect>` | Starts an AI-powered interrogation |
| `inventory` | Displays collected evidence |
| `journal` | Displays investigation progress |
| `analyze` | Requests AI analysis of collected evidence |
| `accuse <suspect>` | Makes the final accusation |
| `quit` | Ends the investigation |

## Example Commands

```text
investigate study
investigate library
investigate laboratory

interrogate rohan
interrogate ananya
interrogate suman
interrogate meera

inventory
journal
analyze

accuse rohan
```

# 🤖 Artificial Intelligence Integration

AI is integrated into the game through the `call_gpt()` function provided by the Stanford Code in Place environment.

The AI is used for three major components.

## 1. Dynamic Suspect Interrogations

Players can ask suspects questions in natural language.

Each suspect has:

- A name and role
- Personality traits
- A motive
- An alibi
- Private character information
- Individual conversation history

The AI generates responses based on the suspect's character and the evidence discovered by the player.

Conversation history is maintained separately for each suspect, allowing interrogations to feel more continuous.

The goal is to create interactions that are more dynamic than predefined dialogue choices.

## 2. Evidence Analysis

The player can use the `analyze` command to request an AI-assisted analysis of collected evidence.

The AI is instructed to:

- Connect discovered clues
- Identify suspicious details
- Separate evidence from speculation
- Highlight unanswered questions
- Suggest useful next investigations
- Avoid revealing the complete solution prematurely

## 3. AI-Generated Epilogue

After the accusation, the AI generates a short narrative epilogue based on the player's investigation.

The epilogue considers:

- Detective score
- Detective rank
- Locations explored
- Suspects interrogated
- Evidence collected
- Investigation outcome

This makes the ending more personalized to the player's investigation.

# 🏆 Scoring System

The game rewards investigation activity.

| Action | Points |
|---|---:|
| Discover a new clue | +10 |
| Interrogate a new suspect | +5 |
| Solve the case with sufficient evidence | Bonus points |

The final score is used to determine the detective's rank.

## Detective Ranks

| Score | Rank |
|---:|---|
| 0–24 | Novice Detective |
| 25–49 | Investigator |
| 50–74 | Senior Investigator |
| 75–99 | Master Detective |
| 100+ | Legendary Detective |

The rank provides a summary of the player's investigative performance.

# ⚖️ Multiple Outcomes

The game supports different investigation outcomes.

### Successful Accusation

The player accuses Rohan and presents sufficient evidence.

The truth is revealed, Rohan confesses, and the case is solved.

### Correct Suspect, Insufficient Evidence

The player identifies Rohan but does not collect enough important evidence.

The suspicion is correct, but the evidence chain is insufficient.

The case remains unresolved.

### Incorrect Accusation

The player accuses an innocent suspect.

The accusation fails, and the real murderer remains free.

These outcomes encourage careful investigation instead of random guessing.
